# Decision log

## D-001 — Treat the robotics thesis as the north star

- **Date:** 2026-08-04
- **Decision:** Treat robots as AI's interface to observe, act in, and learn from the physical world. The project exists to advance this direction, not to repeatedly relitigate it.
- **Correction retained:** A body alone does not create self-improvement. Goals, action-grounded prediction, verification, data selection, learning, safety, and deployment must form a closed loop.

## D-002 — Rank by problem importance before personal fit

- **Date:** 2026-08-04
- **Decision:** Choose work by its effect on the embodied self-improvement bottleneck. Existing skills may alter the route and probability of success, but not the definition of what matters.

## D-003 — Make the Embodied Iteration Engine the first flagship bet

- **Date:** 2026-08-04
- **Decision:** Build an open loop from rollout capture to success detection, failure diagnosis, corrective-data selection, policy update, regression testing, and deployment.
- **Why:** Independent frontier efforts now converge on learning from deployment, reward/success models, online corrections, and action-grounded evaluation. This is the gradient required for recursive improvement.
- **Revisit when:** Evidence shows that the loop does not improve task success or data efficiency across at least one simulated and one physical task.

## D-004 — Stay embodiment-agnostic

- **Date:** 2026-08-04
- **Decision:** Humanoids are important but not doctrine. The engine should work first on a tractable arm or simulation and preserve interfaces for humanoids, mobile manipulators, quadrupeds, and laboratory robots.

## D-005 — Keep personal context private

- **Date:** 2026-08-04
- **Decision:** Public documents contain the thesis, research decisions, and reproducible evidence. Personal facts remain in the private memory repository and are consulted locally when needed.

## D-006 — MicroDuck runs on USB-C 5 V with no soldered power wiring

- **Date:** 2026-10-13
- **Decision:** The robot's supply is a USB-C PD charger into an Adafruit 5991 (HUSB238) set to 5 V on the bench, a 22 AWG solid pair screwed into the OpenRB-150 J4 terminal, and two jumper wires sharing those screws for the Raspberry Pi. No converter, XT30, 12 V adapter or power soldering on the robot. Contract and review-passed picture: `microduck-sim/bringup/build/power-diagram/`.
- **Why:** The earlier 20 V and 12 V chains existed only to give headroom over *simulated* walking peaks (about 2 A mean, 5.4 A for 20 ms at 5 V) against the 3 A limit of USB-C PD at 5 V, and every converter route forced soldering on bare pads. Bring-up draws far less than 3 A. Measured evidence, not a simulation, decides whether a converter comes back.
- **Revisit when:** the guide's `current` step (OpenRB `current_watch.py`) or a charger brown-out shows the 5 V/3 A budget is exceeded. First response is the 5991 `ISet` cut (3.25 A request); only then a converter in the pack channel kept empty for it, with the 5991 switched to 12 V.

## D-007 — MicroDuck servo rail runs at 7.0 V from an onboard XL4015, Pi on its own 5 V

- **Date:** 2026-10-14
- **Decision:** Supersedes D-006's 5 V rail. The robot is powered through one USB-C PD charger into the Adafruit 5991 set to 15 V, then two ROBOTIS OMX-AI kit XL4015 buck modules: one adjusted to 6.8 V (the "7 V rail": 6.75–6.85 V accepted) into the OpenRB-150 J4 (JP1 on VIN(DXL), every servo on that rail through the DXL ports), one adjusted to 5.0 V for the Raspberry Pi's two jumper leads. The three boards ride on the robot in a new two-piece rear frame and lid that replaces the pack cover, flush with the shells and no taller than them (layout F, `microduck-sim/guide/print/back-plate/layout-D-20261014/layout-F/`): both XL4015 lie horizontal and stacked (7 V on top, 5 V in the middle, IN ends on +y, OUT ends on −y), the 5991 lies at the bottom in a narrowed tier with its USB-C socket out of the −y wall; every screw terminal has a side window and its wires loop out and back into a lane behind the boards, so all runs are inside the box and reach the pack through one front passage; outer edges are rounded to follow the shells. The printed V1.8 pack stays as built. Layouts C2 (vertical boards, box raised above the shells), D (horizontal boards with no internal path to the IN terminals) and E (vertical boards, top windows) were rendered and rejected on looks and wiring on 2026-10-14. Still no soldering, no converter in the pack channel, no fuses. Two cables reach the floor: USB-C power and the OpenRB USB data cable.
- **Why:** The official Pollen policies that we run (walk61/stand61 are byte-identical to `BEST_alpha_walking.onnx` / `BEST_alpha_stand.onnx` from pollen-robotics/microduck-simulator) were trained with the bus voltage randomised in 6.5–8.2 V (`microduck_rl/src/mjlab_microduck/robot/microduck_constants.py:126`), and Pollen's robot feeds the XL330s straight from a 7.2 V NP-F550 pack (pollen-robotics/microduck issue 294). At 5 V the as-built simulation tolerates only 2.5 mm of forward COM error; at 7.4 V, 7.5 mm. A 6.8 V set point is inside the training range and 0.15 V under the XL330's Max Voltage Limit default (EEPROM 32 = 70, 7.0 V), so no servo register changes, the factory Shutdown value stays, and meter error or no-load overshoot cannot trip the over-voltage latch. The XL330-M288 e-Manual rates 3.7–6.0 V; running above it is Pollen's production practice, accepted here with the 7.0 V ceiling.
- **Order of work:** the free fixes come first (closed-loop takeover instead of the 1.5 s open-loop ramp, joint-zero and IMU-mount corrections from the three bench measurements), then the 7 V plate. Retraining is not on the plan unless both the official policy and the AI-FanGe community policy fail on hardware at 7 V with logged torque saturation.
- **Revisit when:** a servo reports Input Voltage Error or overheats at 7 V, the 5991/charger browns out at 15 V/3 A, or the XL4015 cannot hold 7.0 V under the measured walking peaks (`current` step).

## D-008 — MicroDuck audio: no GAIN resistor, software volume ceiling; speaker on foam tape

- **Date:** 2026-10-14
- **Decision:** The MAX98357A amplifier's GAIN and SD holes stay empty (default 9 dB gain); the 100 kΩ GAIN-to-Vin resistor planned on 2026-10-13 is dropped. The 1 W CMS-30204-18SP speaker is protected by the ALSA softvol ceiling `max_dB -6.0` in `/etc/asound.conf`, and programs play through the default device, never `hw:MAX98357A`. The speaker sits on a closed loop of 1 mm foam double-sided tape (two strips of about 40 x 1.5 mm) on the cap collar's lip ring instead of a cut foam ring; the speaker's factory metal grille faces the roof and does not replace the tape, which clamps the speaker and seals its edge. Bring-up step 13 is now five soldered wires, two speaker leads, meter check, Pi pins, Pi setup, listening test and the ceiling.
- **Why:** The datasheet gives output level = input (dBFS) + 2.1 dB + gain, so 9 dB with a −6 dB ceiling puts 0.4 W into 8 Ω at full scale, exactly what the 3 dB resistor setting gave at full scale. The resistor cost the only fiddly steps in the guide (lead forming, a hook round the Vin wire tail, Kapton, a paper shim, two meter readings) for no extra protection as long as the ceiling is in place. Shawn has no foam sheet; the 2026-10-13 handoff already allowed foam tape.
- **Revisit when:** a program is found opening the hardware device directly (then the resistor comes back as a hardware floor: about 1.6 W at full scale without it), or the ceiling does not read −6.00 dB on the real Pi.

## D-009 — MicroDuck converter box: four hidden cap screws, solid front plate, wires over the top through a notch

- **Date:** 2026-10-14, cap retention revised 2026-10-15
- **Decision:** The layout F rear converter box (D-007) is released as V1.16 with three changes Shawn chose. The cap has no screw ears, tongues or hook: it pushes straight on and four stock M2 × 10 hold it, each driven from a hole in the cap back down a column inside the cap. Two go through the 7 V XL4015's top corner holes into the frame bosses and two through the Adafruit 5991's upper mounting holes into its standoff bosses. The front plate is solid: the wire passage P1 between the converter tiers is deleted. Every box wire leaves its terminal straight out through the side window beside it and runs outside the wall; the 7 V servo pair and the Pi 5 V lead climb the outside of the left wall, cross the box top and drop into the rear pack through a 9.2 mm notch at the front edge of the top wall, under the side-shell lip, hidden in the crevice between the box and the shell.
- **Why:** Shawn fitted the V1.11 geometry on the robot and found the passage made assembly hard and left a seam between the boards ("开了个洞不仅难装 而且两个板子中间又加了一条缝"), and the cap's screw ear looked like a tail. Routing the wires over the top keeps the box outline clean and needs no fold-back inside the box; the hidden screws reuse a stocked M2 length and existing board bosses, so the cap needs no clip features (Shawn, 2026-10-15: "能放螺丝为什么不放"). The regenerated frame passes the layout checks (manifold, fit, wires, hip sweep with 9.25 mm minimum wire clearance, printability, insertion) and the guide's print gate.
- **Revisit when:** the printed V1.16 cap does not seat flush on the rim with its four screws, or the four wires do not pass the notch under the shell lip with the 22 AWG pairs and the Pi jumper chains actually in hand.


## D-010 — MicroDuck battery backpack: 2S LiPo straight to the servos, DFR1202 for the A7Z inside the backpack

- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Decision:** A second, swappable "battery backpack" joins the D-007 bench backpack, which stays as built. It holds a Tattu 2S 650 mAh 75C XT30 pack, a REV-31-1387 XT30 switch and the owned DFRobot DFR1202. The switched battery reaches the OpenRB-150 J4 through an XT30 joint with no converter, so every servo runs on 6.6-8.4 V; the DFR1202, fed from the same switched line, makes 5.0 V for the Radxa Cubie A7Z header (pins 2 and 6) through an Amass MR30 joint. The robot carries only a male XT30 servo inlet on J4 and a male MR30 on its A7Z lead; each backpack carries the two females, and the bench backpack's XL4015 #2 now feeds the A7Z through its own MR30. Swapping backpacks is two plugs. The DFR1202 `VIN`/`GND` pads carry the servo branch (feed lead in, servo lead out) as well as the 5 V lead. Before the first battery power every servo's Shutdown register (address 63) goes from 53 to 52, and a battery guard warns at 7.0 V and stops at 6.6 V. Plan: `microduck-sim/build-pack-20260913/power/battery-backpack-20261015/README.md`.
- **Why:** Shawn asked to move the robot off the desk supply with a ~7 V battery, one regulated branch for the A7Z and one straight to the OpenRB, and chose the DFR1202 so the 5991 can stay in the bench backpack; on 2026-10-15 he placed the DFR1202 inside the battery backpack and kept XL4015 #2 for the A7Z on the bench. A 2S pack sits inside the 6.5-8.2 V range the walk61/stand61 policies were trained on; Pollen runs the XL330s straight from a 2S NP-F550 with Shutdown 52, because a full pack exceeds the 7.0 V Max Voltage Limit ceiling. The simulated walking draw is about 1.3 A from the pack (about 24 min per 650 mAh pack, 14 min with doubled servo current), with an 8.3 A peak inside the pack's 75C rating. MR30 is used for the 5 V joint because its shape cannot mate with XT30, so battery voltage cannot reach the A7Z. This supersedes D-007's "no servo register changes" for battery operation only.
- **Revisit when:** the measured walking draw exceeds about 2 A (runtime under 15 min: GNB 930 mAh or a deeper backpack), a servo reports Input Voltage Error or overheats on a full pack, the A7Z header reads under 4.85 V, or the DFR1202 pad joints warm up during the walking current test.

## D-012 — MicroDuck audio ceiling raised from −6 dB to −3 dB

- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Decision:** The ALSA softvol ceiling in `/etc/asound.conf` on the duck Pi is `max_dB -3.0` (was −6.0 under D-008), with the daily level stored at 100 %. GAIN stays unconnected (9 dB), programs still play through the default device, and the ceiling never goes above −3 dB. Duck sounds are real recordings (Wikimedia Commons mallard XC62258, CC BY-SA 3.0, or the Pollen microduck-simulator voices), high-passed at 500 Hz below the speaker's 700 Hz resonance and limited to −1 dBFS.
- **Why:** On the first test of the installed speaker Shawn found the quacks too quiet at the −6 dB ceiling, and a full-scale 1 kHz tone at that ceiling sounded clean. At −3 dB a full-scale sine puts 0.8 W into the 8 Ω speaker, inside its 1.0 W rating and well inside the amplifier's 1.4 W at 1 % THD at 5 V; a quack with −9 dBFS RMS averages about 0.2 W. A file-sink measurement through the same softvol on the Pi shows 100 % applies −3.00 dB. The missing GAIN resistor does not cause distortion: with the ceiling the speaker receives the same signal either way.
- **Revisit when:** the speaker buzzes or distorts in the closed head at 100 %, its voice coil smells or warms, or a program is found opening `hw:MAX98357A` directly (about 1.6 W at full scale).

## D-013 — MicroDuck touch sensing: copper pads inside the cap, read by a XIAO ESP32-S3 with soldered wires

- **Date:** 2026-10-15
- **Decision:** Human-pet touch sensing uses five copper foil pads stuck to the inner face of the head cap (L1 and R1 18 mm square, L2, R2 and M 12 mm), read through the 1.7 mm PLA wall by the capacitive touch inputs of a Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 (D0, D1, D2, D3, D8). The XIAO sits on 1 mm foam double-sided tape on the inner left wall of the cap; no printed part changes. Its nine wires are soldered straight to its edge pads, an exception Shawn approved like the amplifier (2026-10-13). It reports to the A7Z over I2C as a slave: SCL on pin 29 (PB2), SDA on pin 31 (PB3), 3.3 V from pin 17, GND on pin 25, all inner-row Dupont plugs. No stock Radxa overlay serves pins 29/31 on the A7Z, so the bench test writes a custom overlay (TWI0 on PB2/PB3, or i2c-gpio on the same pins) at 100 kHz with internal pull-ups. Push is read from the head servos' current or position error at no extra cost.
- **Why:** FSRs need about 0.2 N to switch and preload on a curved shell, while stroking is about 0.4 N spread out; capacitance through the wall needs no force and no hole. A header with Dupont plugs stands about 20 mm and does not fit any pocket in the head; a 21 x 18 mm board fits only on the left-rear wall. The A7Z has no ADC, and the camera covers the edge-row plug ends of pins 10 to 34, so the link must use inner-row pins. Of the overlays Radxa marks compatible with the A7Z, none serves a free inner-row pair: TWI7 and UART3 are on the IMU pins, UART2 on the OpenRB pins, UART4 and TWI2 need covered edge-row pins, and SPI1 needs CS0 on the covered pin 24. Sharing TWI7 with the IMU would join cap-side wires to the torso lead, so the cap could not come off. (`sun60iw2p1-twi0` puts TWI0 on pins 29/31 only for the CM-A7 on the RPi CM5 IO board.) The print gate passed with the parts in the guide on 2026-10-15 (176 parts, 80 steps, 37 cables, no unclassified overlap, no path collision, no mid-route cable penetration).
- **Revisit when:** a fingertip through the wall gives less than five times the resting noise, head roll or yaw reads as touch, the A7Z 3.3 V rail sags below 3.2 V with the XIAO running, or the custom I2C overlay on pins 29/31 does not load or drops reads at 50 Hz.

## D-015 — MicroDuck beak pickup bring-up: Pollen ground-pick policy, Mac-side perception, jaw in current mode

- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Decision:** The first camera-guided pickup is a scripted chain of four components on the existing runtime, built as component bring-up under PROJECT-CONTEXT's scope rule (the learned pickup remains the goal; this chain is its acceptance harness, not the product). (1) Crouch: `walk_wired.py` gains mode `groundpick`, which runs Pollen's `alpha_ground_pick.onnx` unchanged (copied to `runtime-overlay/models/groundpick61.onnx`, sha ffbf5109…, recorded in `source-lock.json`) with the same 61-input layout as walk61/stand61 and the phase command [cos 2πφ, sin 2πφ, 0]; the file command `{"mode":"groundpick","phase":φ}` ramps the phase at 1/4 per s, φ 0.33 is the lowest mouth pose, φ 1.0 rises and hands back to stand61 (KP 200, tilt guard 55°, a trip holds the last goals). (2) Head: walk61/stand61 accept the head-pose command they were trained with (`"head": {neck_pitch, head_pitch, head_yaw, head_roll}` in model radians, clipped to the trained ±1.10/1.10/1.40/0.31 rad deltas, EMA 0.2). (3) Jaw: ID15 joins the sync read; `jaw_ctl.py` runs it in Operating Mode 5 with Goal Current 100 mA, Current Limit 150 mA, every goal through `pi/jaw_limits.py`; the closed/open ticks are marked by hand once into `pi/jaw_calibration.json` (`"jaw_mark"`), `"jaw":"grip"` aims 3° past closed and a stop 3° or more from closed counts as holding. (4) Perception on the Mac: the Pi streams H.264 640x480 at 10 fps (`duck-cam.service`, full-sensor binned mode, flicker period 8333 µs), `orange_target.py` finds the largest orange blob (HSV 5..22/120..255/120..255), `target_ground.py` projects the pixel to the floor through the as-built MuJoCo model's camera and mouth-tip sites with the IMU gravity vector, and `pickup.py` runs look → search (head yaw right first, then body turns) → walk bursts with re-measurement → crouch → grip → rise, sending only the file commands above.
- **Why:** Pollen's ground-pick policy already produces a stable crouch on this geometry: as-built sim at 7.4 V holds φ 0.33 for 4 s with the mouth tip 22 mm over the floor and 69 mm ahead of the mid-foot point, trunk 35°, rises to 0.3° tilt, no fall (`brain_sim_check.py --model asbuilt --volt 7.4 --gp-at 4 --gp-phase 0.33 --gp-hold-s 4`); training our own crouch would violate the no-training-without-go rule and the literature-first rule for no gain at bring-up. The head command costs nothing: the shipped policies were trained with it (as-built sim: yaw 0.6 + pitch 0.6 commanded → 0.67/0.69 reached, tilt 4.7°). The jaw stays in current mode because the phase 1 runtime commissioned it that way and the limits module already exists; a position-mode grip would crush or stall. Perception runs on the Mac because the Pi Zero W has no free CPU (memory: 0 % idle) and no picamera2/cv2; the stream costs it nothing measurable (IMU sample age p95 27.8 ms streaming vs 30.1 ms idle over 60 s). The live chain was checked with the duck standing: 10 fps received, the orange part in view detected in 57/57 frames and projected at x 0.358 m, y 0.224 m, steady to 1 mm; whether that is the true floor position waits on the pitch calibration below. Plumbing tests: `test_pickup_modes.py` (8), `test_pickup.py` (7), `test_target_ground.py` (4), existing `test_walk_wired_battery.py` still pass.
- **Not yet shown:** nothing in this chain has moved the robot. Open before the first run: the real camera pitch differs from CAD (`pickup.py --calibrate D` with the object at a measured distance), the jaw ticks are unmarked, the object must stand at least 4 cm tall and not share the duck's print orange, and the real crouch height under servo sag is unmeasured. Procedure and numbers: `microduck-sim/guide/firmware/mac/PICKUP.md`.
- **Revisit when:** the groundpick policy tips the duck past 55° or fails to rise on the real servos, the jaw cannot hold a 4 cm object at 100 mA, the floor projection is off by more than 3 cm at 0.3 m after calibration, or the learned pickup policy replaces this chain.
- **Amended 2026-10-16 (evening):** the chain ran on the robot piece by piece and then as a closed loop in simulation. Shown on the robot (Shawn guarding): stand61 with head commands (0.3/0.6/0.8 rad commanded → 0.41/0.64/0.82 reached, tilt unchanged), jaw calibration by hand (closed 2042, open 2442 ticks = 35°, `pi/jaw_calibration.json`), open to 20.1° and a finger gripped at 14.1° with HOLDING reported, the groundpick crouch at φ 0.33 (tilt 37°, legs and neck within 0.1 rad of the sim pose) and the rise back to stand61 at 2.6° tilt, the camera projecting a 9 cm object at 0.285 m with a fitted pitch offset of 9.25° (Shawn stopped calibration there: the camera will be swapped). Two things went wrong. (1) A restart of `walk_wired.py` with the control file at `stand` threw every joint half a turn: the first sync read after a start misses every servo, and the Brain ramped from the zero ticks (±π); right-leg servos browned out and rebooted, the head and neck servos overload-latched. The Brain now waits for a fresh joint and IMU sample before entering any torque mode (`WAIT_FOR_FRESH`, two regression tests); latched overloads cleared with the bus Reboot instruction, no power cycle. (2) The pickup loop's walking fell twice: bursts at 0.10–0.15 m/s with the head commanded down diverged within 0.8–1.5 s. The as-built sim afterwards showed walk61 does not step below vx 0.25, does not turn in place, does not walk backwards and stops stepping while a head command is active. Shawn then stopped robot work ("build the framework, make it run in simulation, no need to chase real-robot tests"). The controller was rewritten to those limits (0.25 m/s only, head at its default pose while walking, heading as a yaw rate held over the burst, forward arcs instead of turns, re-stand on stand61 after a guard trip) and a closed-loop simulation harness built (`pickup_sim.py`: as-built model at 7.4 V, the same Brain, policies and `Pickup` machine, the head camera rendered with an orange box, the same detector and projection; only the grip is faked). With the sim's own fitted pitch offset (+4.75°, the model's IMU mount sits 4° off the trunk) all three test placements end standing with the object lifted to 0.18 m and no fall: right-front (0.50, −0.25) in 170 s and 23 bursts, ahead (0.35, 0) in 225 s and 31 bursts, right 60° (0.35, −0.60) in 28 s and 4 bursts. The time goes into misses: walk61 stops in whatever stride phase it is in, so the same 2 s burst moves 1–21 cm, the pickup window is a 3 cm circle, and a miss costs a 35–50 s search loop. The projection now uses the blob's base rather than its centroid (a 9 cm box's centroid sits 4 cm outward of its footprint at 15 cm), and the neck lifts only to about −0.09 rad whatever the command, so the nearest floor the camera sees on stand61 is 6 cm ahead. The rewritten walking has not been run on the robot. Procedure, numbers and traces: `microduck-sim/guide/firmware/mac/PICKUP.md` and `pickup-sim-20261016/`.


## D-016 — MicroDuck walk61 fine-tuning: reward audit gate and task-metric guard before any Modal run

- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Decision:** Every continued-training run of a shipped MicroDuck policy passes two code gates in `microduck-sim/microduck_rl/modal/`. (1) Launch gate: `finetune.py::run` refuses to spawn unless `reward-audit.json` holds a PASS for the run's environment variant, written by `finetune.py::audit`: Pollen's stand61 and walk61 are rolled out deterministically in the exact training env (2048 envs, 15 s) and the reward per simulated second is tallied by term; the variant passes only when the standing policy collects at most the audited fraction of the walking policy's reward (threshold calibrated so the env that produced walk61 passes and the env that produced ft1 fails). Deltas must be registered (`DELTAS`) to be audited; there is no skip flag. (2) Training guard: the worker averages the command manager's velocity-tracking error between saves, takes the critic warm-up (frozen walk61) as the baseline, excludes saves above `--track-guard` x baseline from `model_best.pt` and stops after two such saves; the PPO learning rate is clamped (`--max-lr`). Total reward is logged but never used as a stop or selection criterion. Checkpoints are exported with `modal/ckpt2onnx.py` and judged in the as-built MuJoCo suite (`guide/firmware/mac/brain_sim_check.py --model asbuilt`, seven cases) against walk61 before any robot test.
- **Why:** Run ft1 (Modal app ap-K5AexZpPzN7XaskCaq8mJy, L4, 999 iterations, 56 min, $1.05) doubled the action-rate penalty and widened friction, supply-sag, noise and push randomization; PPO moved the policy to standing still from iteration 225 (as-built suite: 10 s distance 0.003–0.009 m in all seven cases against walk61's 1.4–2.2 m) while total reward rose 27.6 → 98.8 and the tracking error rose 0.28 → 0.46. The reward-based guard could not fire because total reward was the hacked quantity. The audit of Pollen's own env with as-built masses shows why the design is fragile: standing already collects 6.01 of walking's 7.48 reward per second (80 %); `track_linear_velocity` pays standing 1.324/s against walking's 1.329/s, so `air_time` (+1.0/s) is the only term that pays stepping, and anything that taxes stepping by about that much flips the optimum. Pollen's note in `microduck_velocity_env_cfg.py` (run 5yay13u4) records the same flip from a tightened head tolerance. Tracking error at iteration 110 (0.43 against 0.28) would have stopped ft1 after about $0.15.
- **Revisit when:** the audit threshold passes an env that later produces a standing policy, or fails an env in which a policy demonstrably keeps walking; or the reward design itself changes (a tighter velocity-tracking std or a stepping bonus would change the margin the audit measures).

## D-017 — MicroDuck walk61 fine-tune ft3: velocity-tracking reward filtered and tightened ("lowcmd" delta)

- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Decision:** The next continued-training run of walk61 (`microduck_rl/modal`, delta `lowcmd`) replaces Pollen's `track_linear_velocity` (Gaussian, std² 0.1, on the instantaneous base velocity) by the same Gaussian on a 0.5 s exponential moving average of the base velocity with std² 0.05 (`track_linear_velocity_ema`, weight 2.0 unchanged), on top of the ft2 staged randomization. Air time (3.0), the standing-env fraction (0.25), the 61-input layout, KP 200 and the per-step tracking guard are unchanged. Audit `audit-1016-2046` (2048 envs × 15 s, as-built): stand/walk 0.798, walk61 falls 0.36 per env-minute, margin 1.457/s (staged 1.371/s), PASS. The run goes only under a cost cap Shawn approves in that session.
- **Why:** In the as-built CPU model walk61 under a 0.25 m/s command moves at 0.12 m/s with a 0.17 m/s lateral and 0.08 m/s vertical stride oscillation; Pollen's instantaneous Gaussian prices that oscillation, so the term pays walking 1.23/s against standing 1.07/s at that command (audit over all commands: 1.32 vs 1.35), and ft1/ft2 drifted to standing. On the same traces the filtered term pays 1.37 vs 0.57/s. It is the structure Pollen already documented for the head (`head_pose_bias`: price the escapable DC error on an EMA). The literature pass (`FINETUNE-DELTA.md`, ft3): legged_gym and Open Duck Mini v2 use a Gaussian 2.5-10× tighter relative to the command range (Open Duck: σ² 0.01 at ±0.15 m/s), and Humanoid-Gym, Siekmann 2021 and van Marum 2024 hold stepping with a term independent of velocity tracking (gait clock, imitation, air time with a touchdown charge); a clock input would change the observation layout and is excluded.
- **Outcome (2026-10-17):** ft3 (app ap-xa8A5RNwW2qTAk1FmSdvRJ, L4, 999 iterations, 56 min, $1.04) did not drift: speed along the command 0.099 → 0.144 m/s, low-command speed 0.079 → 0.111, tracking error 0.278 → 0.258, guard never fired. As-built suite at the robot's real sensor condition (IMU full 50 Hz, no dropped servo reads): iteration 999 never falls, walks 24-43 % further than walk61, steps at 0.20 m/s where walk61 does not, with about 1° more peak tilt and 1-9 vs 1-2 action jumps per 10 s. Under harsher stress cases (IMU held at 35-45 Hz, 8 % dropped reads) it makes 2-4× walk61's jumps; those cases are not the robot's condition. Delivered as `runtime-overlay/models-ft3-it999/` (walk61.onnx = iteration 999); candidate for a robot comparison against walk61, not yet run on the robot. Rerun at the recorded walking rail (7.0 V, from `microduck-sim/CURRENT-STATE.json`, previously a hardcoded 7.4 V): real cases unchanged in verdict (no falls, 17-69 % further); in one 35 Hz + 8 % drop stress seed it999 falls where walk61 does not.
- **Revisit when:** ft3 drifts to standing under this term (then the standing-env fraction during fine-tuning, 0.25 → 0.10, is the next registered delta), or the as-built suite shows the filtered term changed the gait (bounce, slip, shorter air time) at 0.25-0.30 m/s.
