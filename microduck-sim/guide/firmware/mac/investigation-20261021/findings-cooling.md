## BLUF

1. The resets are not explained by a kernel thermal shutdown. Radxa's own BSP device tree puts the CPU critical trip at 110 °C (throttling from 70 °C, target 90 °C); reset 4 happened at 86 °C and the board came back by itself 26 s later. A 5 V dip on the XL4015 #2 branch remains the leading untested cause; the cold/hot load test with a meter on pins 2/6 plus `journalctl -b -1` / `last -x` at the next power-up separates them.
2. Heat still costs control rate even without resets: on 10/21 the loop p50 rose from 14.1 to 21.8 ms in 12 minutes and the tick rate fell to 43.7/s (below 50 Hz). Fixing that needs less power first (software), then better coupling of the chip to air (heatsink), then an air path (vents).
3. Do now: 15x15x5 mm copper heatsink with fins vertical, keep the ORT one-thread fix, test a fixed CPU cap or big cores offline, and log both CPU clusters. Vents ride on the next head-cap reprint (usb-service slot + roof band A), only after Shawn sees the picture. No face reprint is needed for the notches; if the face is reprinted, drop the notches and add the planned LED window.
4. The official Open Duck Mini v2 head has no cooling opening and no heatsink/fan in its BOM; nothing there needs copying.
5. Zero 2 W is not a fix: not buyable now (D-011), about 3-3.7x less CPU, and it also throttles in a closed case (PiCockpit: 81 °C, throttled, official case).

### Trip points and governor (vendor source)
- Radxa BSP device tree (same file, lines 527-590; identical in branches v1.4.8 and v1.5.0): `cpul_thermal_zone` and `cpub_thermal_zone` each have trips at 70 °C passive (threshold), 90 °C passive (target, the cooling map acts here) and 110 °C critical; `sustainable-power` 756 (little) and 914 (big) mW, i.e. the power-allocator governor. Idle-injection zones at 90/100 °C. GPU first trip 60 °C. Whether the installed image uses these values is not verified (check below).
- Radxa product brief Rev 1.1 §6 (https://dl.radxa.com/cubie/a7z/docs/radxa_cubie_a7z_product_brief.pdf, scratch text lines 239-247): "The internal governor ensures the CPU temperature does not exceed 85°C"; "operates reliably without additional cooling for typical use. For sustained heavy workloads ... additional cooling may be required"; ambient 0-50 °C.
- Allwinner A733 datasheet Table 6-2/6-3 (p.120): Tj recommended max 115 °C, absolute 125 °C; θJA 25.265 °C/W, θJB 10.33 °C/W (JEDEC still air, reference only).
- Radxa forum "Extreme throttling introduced on A733" (https://forum.radxa.com/t/extreme-throttling-introduced-on-a733/30688): user reports throttling to about 400 MHz above 60 °C; Radxa staff: throttling is unavoidable, under 30 min stress the SoC still shuts down at 100+ °C without cooling, small heatsinks help, the rsetup user_space governor disables thermal control. Another user: a 6x6 cm passive heatsink keeps the CPU under 95 °C at 20 °C ambient.
- Radxa quick start (https://docs.radxa.com/en/cubie/a7z/getting-started/quickly-start): passive cooling is limited and the SoC "may overheat and cause system instability ... under prolonged or heavy workloads"; "We recommend installing an appropriate heatsink".
- Power (third party): LinuxLinks measured the A7Z at 5.4 W under 100 % CPU stress (https://www.linuxlinks.com/radxa-cubie-a7z-single-board-computer-running-linux-power-consumption/2/); idle 2.2 W is quoted on the 10/20 cooling page and not re-verified here.

## Q3 Options (effects are inference unless marked)

Rule for judging: the governor holds the chip near its set point, so improvements show up as higher held clocks and shorter loops, not mainly as lower temperatures. Acceptance is a 35-min closed-head soak with loop p90, tick rate and both cluster clocks.

- (a) Heatsink in the closed head. 15x15x5 mm copper (GeeekPi pack, Amazon B0C7Z27Q3R, $12.99 per the 10/20 page, not re-verified) on the A733, not touching the RAM. Fins vertical (channels along the board's up direction); the 10/20 page says any direction is fine, which should be corrected because the channel air moves upward. Effect: adds fin area right at the chip, so the chip-to-head-air resistance falls; expected a few kelvin to about 10 K at the same power, i.e. a higher held clock. Mass +5-10 g. Cheapest and reversible.
- (b) Heatsink plus vents. Chimney estimate: Q = Cd A sqrt(2 g H ΔT/T), Cd 0.6, H 0.05 m (neck inlet to roof), ΔT 15-25 K: about 0.2-0.3 W carried out by a 109 mm² outlet, about 0.4-0.6 W with band A added. The cap walls already shed a few watts, so vents alone are a modest gain (roughly 5-15 % more heat out of the head). They matter more with a fan. Needs a cap reprint (White, roughly 2 h on the X1C as half the V1.17 White plate's volume; not sliced) and a re-stuck or new AC019.
- (c) 5 V micro fan. Only 20 mm class plausibly fits beside the board (pin-40 end has 14.5 mm free); 25/30 mm need a CAD study. Candidates: Adafruit 3368, 30x30x8 mm, 5 V, 0.2 A, 6.2 g, $3.50, in stock (fetched 2026-10-21); The Pi Hut 25x25x7 mm 5 V, £2.50; Sunon 20x20 mm 5 V parts at RS/DigiKey (about $7, DigiKey stock 0 per aggregator). Power: J6 is 0.8 mm pitch, so a generic fan needs a matching plug or a splice to 5 V; a fan adds 0.1-0.2 A to the same 5 V branch that is suspected of dipping, so do it only after the 5 V test. A fan without an outlet only stirs head air.
- (d) Shawn's copper "two-leaf sprout". Route: heatsink top up over the board's top edge and out through roof band A (the roof directly above the channel is the antenna). Conduction estimate: 8x1 mm strip, 45 mm long, about 14 K/W; two leaves of about 470 mm² each, both faces, h about 10 W/m²K, about 53 K/W; total about 70 K/W to room air, so about 0.5-1 W removed. Mass about 7.5 g strip/leaves plus a heatsink up to 10 g; with `stand-gap-20261021/head_mass.py` margins 24.7/23.5/21.7/19.1/14.5 mm at +0/20/50/100/200 g, +10-17 g costs about 0.6-1 mm of toe margin (negligible). Risks: metal a few mm behind the AC019 can detune Wi-Fi; it sits next to touch pad M (x 42-55) and may shift its baseline; leaves at 60-75 °C are hot to touch on a head people pat; the roof hole needs a sealed, printable pass-through. Feasible, modest gain; worth it only as a look Shawn wants, after (a) and (e) are measured.
- (e) Software, against the 50 Hz loop (12-13 ms cool, 20 ms budget). Keep the ORT one-thread fix (it removed about two cores of busy-wait). Log both clusters. Then test, cold and after 35 min closed: (1) a fixed `scaling_max_freq` on the A76 policy (for example 1.2-1.4 GHz) so the governor stops swinging 416<->1794; (2) A76 cores offline (the "关大核" test on the 10/20 page), accepting it only if the loop p90 stays under about 18 ms. Do not use the rsetup user_space governor (Radxa staff: it disables thermal control). Run the camera/detector only when needed and at low fps; check which camera service runs.
- (f) Zero 2 W. Third party: PiCockpit (https://picockpit.com/raspberry-pi/does-the-raspberry-pi-zero-2-require-a-heatsink/, 22 °C, stress-ng 4 cores) bare board 74 °C, official case 81 °C and throttled, FLIRC metal case 62 °C; Raspberry Pi says uncased at 20 °C it runs LINPACK "indefinitely without throttling" thanks to "thick internal copper layers" (https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/new-raspberry-pi-zero-2-w-2/). Compute: sbc-bench 7-zip, Zero 2 W 3030 multi / 838 single vs Cubie A7S (same A733) 11280 / 2480, about 3.7x and 3.0x (https://github.com/ThomasKaiser/sbc-bench/blob/master/Results.md). Why not chosen: D-011 (DECISIONS.md line 72) "The Pi Zero 2 W is unavailable and the head computer must run the walking policy, balance and the camera"; US stock still out on 10/21 per the earlier agent (Adafruit 5291, PiShop, CanaKit). Switching back also drops the 13M 214 (31-pin) for a 22-pin CSI camera and the V1.17 face (inference). Pollen's pre-built runtime image "is currently only tested on the Raspberry Pi Zero 2W" (https://github.com/apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini_Runtime README line 7), so it would run the walking loop, but it would not fix heat in a closed head and gives up the camera headroom.

## Q5 RoboClub and the next power-up

- Inventory (`roboclub/INVENTORY.md` line 73): HEATSINKS drawer has a 40 mm fan cooler and large coolers, thermal paste, a small clear box not checked, and "No small adhesive heatsink (Pi/Zero size)"; line 80: copper foil tape. No meter or oscilloscope listed (inventory is partial).
- Look for: small adhesive heatsinks 15x15 mm or smaller, at most 8 mm tall (check the clear box and any Pi kit); thermal tape or a 0.5-1 mm thermal pad (paste alone needs clamping); 20-30 mm 5 V fans (only for later); a multimeter with MIN/MAX (Peak mode if it has one); an oscilloscope. Buy if absent: GeeekPi B0C7Z27Q3R; optionally Adafruit 3368.
- `a7z_load_test.sh` (lines 8-24): default 20 s; logs to `~/duck-logs/load_test.log` with a sync per line; prints boot_id, all zone temperatures (integer °C), the cpu0 clock and any `power_supply/*/voltage_now` before, every second during and after; spawns `nproc` busy loops that each call `date +%s` until the end time; never touches the servos; same boot_id at the end means no reset. `thermal_log.py`: described above.
- First measurement at the next power-up: (1) before anything else, read the previous boot's end: `journalctl --list-boots`, `journalctl -b -1 -n 80 --no-pager`, `last -x | head` (crash vs shutdown), `ls /var/log/journal` (is the journal persistent), and the trip points `grep . /sys/class/thermal/thermal_zone*/trip_point_*_temp`. (2) Head open, cold: meter MIN/MAX on header pins 2 (+5 V) and 6 (GND), then `~/duck/a7z_load_test.sh 20`. (3) Hot: cap on (leads out through the seam), 35 min with the thermal log running, then the same 20 s test. Read: min >= 4.9 V and same boot_id = no reproduction; < 4.75 V = the 5 V branch; a reset with the meter steady at temperatures far below 110 °C = a fast dip a meter cannot see or a board-level fault, so repeat with an oscilloscope single trigger at 4.75 V falling. A DMM MIN/MAX samples a few times per second and misses millisecond dips (the 10/20 page says the same).
