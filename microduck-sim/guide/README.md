# MicroDuck build guide (current)

This folder is the single current entry point for finishing the MicroDuck: the remaining prints, the step-by-step assembly and wiring guide, and the controller firmware. Older material under `build-pack-20260913/` is history.

## Current design

- **Power (7 V servo rail set to 7.40 V, 15 V from USB-C, no soldering; adopted 2026-10-14, set point changed 2026-10-15, `PROJECT-CONTEXT.json` `current_build.servo_rail_7v_20261014` and `current_build.servo_shutdown_52_20261015`, robotics `DECISIONS.md` D-014, picture and contract in `../bringup/build/power-diagram/README.md`):** USB-C PD charger (Shawn's Xiaomi 67 W: 5/9/15/20 V) → USB-C cable → Adafruit 5991 (HUSB238) with only DIP switch 3 (`15V`) on (exactly one slider: with `5V` also on it negotiates 5 V; the switch is read only at plug-in, so replug after a change; `9V` is not allowed: on 2026-10-15 its 27 W offer browned out the whole robot within 0.5 s of the first walk command) and ISet left closed → its screw terminal → 22 AWG solid pair → XL4015 #1 IN, set to 7.40 V (accept 7.35–7.45 V) → 22 AWG solid pair through the rear power plate into the pack channel → OpenRB-150 terminal block J4 (red +, black −, one wire per screw), JP1 on VIN(DXL); all servos take the 7 V rail through the OpenRB DXL ports. XL4015 #2 IN is chained from #1 IN (two solid wires per #1 IN screw) and set to 5.00 V for the Pi. Both XL4015 are the ROBOTIS OMX-AI kit "DC converter" (61.7 × 26.2 mm, four M2 × 6 each into 1.6 mm pilots; the third kit's unit stays on the OMX-F arm). The two spare units come with the ROBOTIS harness (JST EH plug at each end, ferrules under the screws): it comes off and stays with its kit, and our 22 AWG wires go into the terminals as bare copper, no ferrules and no tinning. The 5991 mounts with four M2 × 6 (plate counts follow the plate CAD). One wire rule: solid 22 AWG for the short fixed runs on the plate and into the pack, stranded for anything that moves or trails. The 5991 and both converters ride on a printed rear power plate behind the pack (`print/back-plate/`, in progress); two cables reach the table: the USB-C power cable from the 5991 at the plate bottom and the OpenRB USB data cable through the floor slot (wired control from the Mac). Disconnect = unplug the USB-C cable. The DFR1202, the 12 V SMPS, the SMPS2DXL, XT30 and fuses are not used.
- **Why 7.4 V on the 7 V rail:** Pollen's walk and stand policies were trained with the XL330 supply between 6.5 and 8.2 V (`../microduck_rl/src/mjlab_microduck/robot/microduck_constants.py`, `vin_range=(6.5, 8.2)`), and Pollen's robot feeds the servos straight from a 7.2 V NP-F550 pack with Shutdown 52 (github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck issue 294). All 15 servos hold Shutdown (address 63) = 52 since 2026-10-15 (`firmware/pi/set_shutdown.py`, 15/15 read back): above the factory Max Voltage Limit (address 32, 70 = 7.0 V) they raise the input-voltage flag and keep torque; overload, overheating and electrical shock still cut torque. 7.40 V is the 2S LiPo nominal voltage the battery backpack delivers; in simulation with the measured 7.87° forward gap 6.8 V stayed upright but drifted back 8 cm, 7.4 V stayed upright, 8.0 V stayed upright and drifted 8.7 cm forward. ROBOTIS rates the XL330-M288 at 3.7–6.0 V. Check with `firmware/pi/check_voltage_limit.py --rail battery` (pass 6.4–8.4 V and Shutdown 52 on all 15; the default `--rail 7v` expects the retired 6.80 V set point). `mac/walk_wired.py --supply auto` arms its battery guard at 7.2 V, which also lets Alert-flagged status packets through above 7.0 V; at 7.35 V it arms, warns under 7.0 V and stops at 6.6 V. XL4015 #2 stays at 5.00 V.
- **Pi 5 V lead:** two jumper chains (30 cm male-female into 30 cm female-female, joint taped) from XL4015 #2 OUT (male pin cut off, 10 mm stripped, twisted and folded back under each screw) to Pi pins 2 (red) and 6 (black). Never 15 V or 7 V on the Pi pins. Limits: OpenRB DXL ports 3 A together (ROBOTIS), XL4015 #1 about 4 A continuous (5 A chip rating; https://protosupplies.com/product/xl4015-adjustable-dc-dc-step-down-module/, https://probots.co.in/xl4015-5a-constant-current-voltage-led-driver-lithium-battery-charger.html), USB side 15 V × 3 A = 45 W (the 9 V offer, 27 W, browned out on walking, 2026-10-15); `firmware/pi/current_watch.py` logs the summed servo current against the 3 A port rating before the first walk.
- **Controller:** OpenRB-150 stands upright in the printed rear electronics pack (`print/rear-pack/`) that hangs on the four hip-yaw case screws in place of the power support: components to the rear, terminal block up, USB-C down over a floor slot (bench access), four M2 × 6 into pads on the pack plate. The 7 V pair comes into the channel behind the board from the rear power plate; the BNO085 stands on the pack's front plate between the hip motors; a black cover closes the back. Its power jumper is on VIN(DXL) and its header tails are trimmed to 1.5 mm.
- **Servo bus:** XL330-M288-T, Protocol 2.0, 1 Mbps. Right leg 5 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 from the lower centre port; left leg 10 → 9 → 8 → 7 → 6 from the upper centre port; neck 12 → 11 → 13 → 14 → 15 from the +Y side port through the pack wall window. The −Y side port is unused. All links are OMX-AI kit X3P cables (180 mm and 100 mm).
- **Leg chains:** four X3P cables per leg, socket by socket: right 5s14→4s13, 4s14→3s14 (100 mm), 3s13→2s14, 2s13→1s13; left 10s14→9s14, 9s13→8s13 (100 mm), 8s14→7s13, 7s14→6s14; the rest 180 mm. Thigh and ankle routes are the 2026-10-09 leg routes (`content/leg_routes.py`). The two hip cables drop through the 12 × 6 mm slot in the middle of the torso base beside the BNO085, because the rear pack fills the pocket where the old route coiled; their spare length folds flat on the hip-yaw motor tops under the shells. Guide chapter 4 checks the legs and plugs any missing cable (thigh plugs only with the thigh servos out, ankle plugs only with the lower link off).
- **Head:** Pi Zero W, Camera Module 3 Wide, MAX98357A (3 dB gain) and speaker. The Pi reaches the OpenRB over UART: Pi pin 8 (TXD) → OpenRB J12 pad 3 (printed Rx), Pi pin 10 (RXD) ← J12 pad 4 (printed Tx), two 26 AWG wires soldered into J12 (Serial2 in the bridge firmware); the J12 V and G pads stay empty. The OpenRB runs `firmware/openrb_uart_bridge`.
- **Neck bundle:** eight thin wires (Pi 5 V lead pair, IMU ×4, UART ×2) leave the pack notch, cross the torso top to the torso clip, run up the right side of the neck through the pitch-bracket clip and enter the head beside the neck into the head clip (V1.9 route, `print/ext-v19/route-v19.json`). About 5 mm of bow between the torso and bracket clips covers head motion: the worst pose over the full joint ranges needs 38.8 mm of slack (`print/ext-v19/checks/route-v19-motion.json`), so each wire is cut at its route length plus 60 mm.

Servo roles: 1 R ankle pitch, 2 R knee, 3 R hip pitch, 4 R hip roll, 5 R hip yaw, 6–10 the same on the left, 11 head pitch, 12 neck pitch, 13 head yaw, 14 head roll, 15 jaw.

## Python environment

`/tmp/microduck-cad-venv` is a symlink to the persistent venv `~/.venvs/microduck-cad` (Python 3.12, uv). Files in `/tmp` that are not read for a few days are deleted, which broke the original venv there on 2026-10-13. Rebuild the venv with `uv venv --python /opt/homebrew/opt/python@3.12/bin/python3.12 ~/.venvs/microduck-cad`, then `uv pip install --python ~/.venvs/microduck-cad/bin/python -r cad-venv-requirements.txt`. If the symlink is missing, recreate it with `ln -s ~/.venvs/microduck-cad /tmp/microduck-cad-venv`.

## Build the guide

```bash
cd ~/code/robotics/microduck-sim
/tmp/microduck-cad-venv/bin/python guide/content/build_guide_data.py
python3 guide/build_guide.py --data guide/data/guide-data.json --out guide/dist/MICRODUCK-BUILD.html
```

Then run the print gate and the design check (both read-only on the design):

```bash
cd ~/code/robotics/microduck-sim/guide/content/interference-audit && /tmp/microduck-cad-venv/bin/python run_gate.py
cd ~/code/robotics/microduck-sim && python3 guide/verify_current_design.py --scope design
```
