## The chain

1. USB-C PD charger (Xiaomi 67 W, offers 5/9/15/20 V) → USB-C cable → Adafruit 5991 (HUSB238 PD board). Only DIP switch **3** (`15V`) ON, the other five OFF; leave the `ISet` jumper closed. Exactly one switch: with `5V` also ON the board negotiates 5 V (Shawn read 5.167 V on 2026-10-15; the 5V switch ties VSET to GND). The board reads the switch only at plug-in, so replug after any change. Terminal reads 14.5–15.5 V. Never `9V`: on 2026-10-15 the 9 V / 3 A offer browned out the whole robot within 0.5 s of the first walk command (all 15 servos lost torque, the OpenRB USB re-enumerated, the Pi went offline; robot log `/tmp/duck_wired_log_1015b.jsonl` read 7.1 V and 5° tilt just before the cut).
2. 5991 screw terminal `+`/`-` → 22 AWG solid red/black pair → XL4015 #2 `IN+`/`IN-`. A second pair daisy-chains from the XL4015 #2 IN screws to XL4015 #1 `IN+`/`IN-`, so both converters see 15 V.
3. XL4015 #1, set to **7.40 V** (accept 7.35–7.45 V; Shawn's robot reads 7.35 V on 2026-10-15) → 22 AWG pair up the outside of the box, over its top and down through the wire notch at the front edge of the top wall into the pack → OpenRB-150 J4 (red `+`, black `-`). JP1 stays on `VIN(DXL)`. Every servo gets this 7 V rail through the three DXL links, unchanged.
4. XL4015 #2, set to **5.00 V** (4.95–5.10 V) → the Pi's two jumper leads (Pi pin 2 red, pin 6 black). The Pi leads leave J4.
5. The 5991 and both XL4015 sit in the rear converter box (layout F, approved 2026-10-14: `guide/print/back-plate/layout-F/`). Seen from behind: XL4015 #1 (7 V) lying flat in the top tier, #2 (5 V) in the middle tier, each with IN at the right (-Y) end and OUT at the left (+Y), trimmers along the lower edge; the 5991 in the narrow bottom tier with its screw terminal to the right and its USB-C socket to the left. On every terminal the `+` screw is the lower one. Each wire leaves its terminal sideways through a window in the side wall, makes a U-loop outside the wall and comes back in to the rear lane under the lid. The 7 V pair and the Pi lead leave through passage P1 (just left of centre, between the two converter tiers) into the pack channel; the USB-C plug enters through the low window in the left-hand (+Y) wall, the robot's left side. The lid is a rounded cap (x -67.1..-59.1) that carries the rear half of each terminal window; it pushes straight on and four hidden M2 x 10 along +x hold it, driven from holes in its back: two through XL4015 #1's top corner holes (y +-28.25, z 38.7) and two through the 5991's upper holes (y +-11.37, z -16.6) into the frame bosses (V1.16, 2026-10-15). Two cables reach the floor: the USB-C power cable and the OpenRB USB data cable. Disconnect = unplug the USB-C power cable.

No soldering anywhere: every power joint is a screw terminal or a Dupont pin.

## Removed

DFR1202 buck converter, ROBOTIS 12 V SMPS, SMPS2DXL and its cable, the Xiaomi 20 V plan, the 5 V USB-C chain of 2026-10-13 (Pi on the J4 screws). UART (Pi pin 8/10 ↔ OpenRB J12), IMU, camera and amplifier wiring are unchanged.

## Why 7.4 V on the 7 V rail

- The walk and stand policies were trained with the servo supply randomised in 6.5–8.2 V (`microduck_rl/src/mjlab_microduck/robot/microduck_constants.py:126`, `vin_range=(6.5, 8.2)`).
- Pollen's own MicroDuck feeds the XL330s straight from a 7.2 V NP-F550 pack (github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck issue 294).
- XL330 Max Voltage Limit: control table address 32, initial 70 = 7.0 V, range 31–70 (https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/x/xl330-m288/). ROBOTIS rates the XL330-M288 at 3.7–6.0 V; running above that is Pollen's practice, accepted here.
- Shutdown (address 63, initial 53) has bit 0 (Input Voltage Error) set, so a factory servo reading above its Max Voltage Limit switches its torque off and latches the error until power-cycled. Pollen's maintainer reports shutdown = 52 on their robot (bit 0 clear), which is why their 7.2–8.4 V pack does not trip it. Since 2026-10-15 all 15 of our servos hold **52** too (`guide/firmware/pi/set_shutdown.py` over the OpenRB USB link, 15/15 read back): above 7.0 V they raise the input-voltage flag and keep torque; overload, overheating and electrical shock still cut it. On a fresh build the guide's `first-power` step writes 52 (torque off, write, power-cycle, check).
- The rail is therefore set to **7.40 V** (accept 7.35–7.45 V), the 2S LiPo nominal voltage the battery backpack delivers, so the bench and the battery feed the servos alike. Simulation with the measured 7.87° forward gap (`hardware-design-20260912/whole-robot-20260912/dynamics/realgap-20261014/README.md` section 7 for the gap): 6.8 V upright but drifted back 8 cm, 7.4 V upright, 8.0 V upright and 8.7 cm forward.
- Check: `guide/firmware/pi/check_voltage_limit.py --rail battery` (read-only) passes at 6.4–8.4 V with Shutdown 52 on every servo; under 7.0 V it warns. The default `--rail 7v` belongs to the retired 6.80 V set point and fails above 7.0 V. `guide/firmware/mac/walk_wired.py --supply auto` arms its battery guard at 7.2 V, which also lets the Alert-flagged status packets through; at 7.35 V it arms, warns under 7.0 V and stops at 6.6 V.
- Until 2026-10-15 the set point was 6.80 V (D-007), 0.15 V under the 7.0 V limit, chosen only to keep the factory Shutdown 53.

## Limits and settings

- **5991 at 15 V.** Silk from the top reads 5V/9V/12V/15V/18V/20V for switches 6→1, so 15V is switch 3; ON = slider toward the labels (https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-usb-type-c-power-delivery-switchable-breakout/pinouts). The HUSB238 asks for the highest source PDO with voltage ≤ the set voltage and current ≥ the ISet current; the chip on the 5991 (marking 003DD) falls back to the next lower PDO when no match exists (HUSB238 datasheet pp. 9 and 14, https://cdn-learn.adafruit.com/assets/assets/000/125/150/original/husb238_datasheet_full.pdf). So a charger without 15 V gives 12 V or 9 V; read the meter before connecting anything. **Keep `ISet` closed** (1.25 A request): cut, it requests 3.25 A, which a 15 V/3 A PDO cannot meet, and the board falls back to a lower voltage.
- **Power budget.** 15 V × 3 A = 45 W from the charger's 15 V PDO. At about 90 % converter efficiency that is roughly 5.5 A at 7.4 V for servos and Pi together (calculation, efficiency assumed), so the OpenRB's 3 A port rating stays the tighter limit. The 9 V PDO (27 W, about 3.3 A at 7.4 V by the same calculation) did not carry walking peaks: the 2026-10-15 brownout above. With 15 V in, XL4015 #1 still reads 7.35 V and the servos read 7.1–7.2 V (`check_voltage_limit.py --rail battery` PASS, Shawn, 2026-10-15). The guide's `first-power` and `current` steps read the OpenRB terminal under load.
- **XL4015.** 8–36 V in, 1.25–32 V out, 5 A switch, 0.3 V minimum dropout (XL4015 datasheet excerpt, https://www.radiolocman.com/datasheet/data.html?di=685191); modules are rated about 4 A continuous, 4.5 A maximum, with the output at least about 1.5 V below the input (protosupplies.com XL4015 module page, https://protosupplies.com/product/xl4015-adjustable-dc-dc-step-down-module/). Shawn's kit board is the CC/CV type: two trimmers, one for voltage (CV) and one for current limit (CC), clockwise raises each; red LED = current-limit mode; no reverse-polarity protection (probots.co.in XL4015 CC/CV page, https://probots.co.in/xl4015-5a-constant-current-voltage-led-driver-lithium-battery-charger.html). With OUT left, IN right and the trimmers at the bottom, the right trimmer next to the XL4015 chip U1 should be CV and the left one next to the LM358 CC (`../../parts/photos/xl4015_trimmers_annotated.png`; inferred from the layout, not yet bench-confirmed). The guide's `bench-test` step and bring-up step 12 keep the turn-and-watch meter test as the deciding check.
- **Kit harness.** The two spare kit XL4015 come with the ROBOTIS harness screwed in (JST EH plug at each end, ferrules under the screws). Unscrew it from both terminals and keep it with its kit; our 22 AWG wires go in as bare copper, no ferrules and no tinning. Solid 22 AWG for the short fixed runs (15 V feed and link, 7 V pair), stranded for anything that moves or trails.
- **Set with no load.** Both converters are trimmed with nothing on OUT, read back after a full replug, and only then wired to J4 and the Pi.
- **Current check.** Guide step `current` (`guide/firmware/pi/current_watch.py`) sums XL330 Present Current; the OpenRB DXL ports share a 3 A rating.
