# MicroDuck compute and interface review

Checked 2026-09-13. Advances P0, P5 and P6. The acceptance target remains camera-guided target finding, alignment, learned jaw control, lifting and independent success assessment. The completed jaw experiment remains the starting point.

## Decision

Keep the Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W on the duck for the existing 50 Hz balance/motor loop, BNO085 orientation sensor, camera capture and audio. Use the existing MacBook Pro for visual target estimation, experiment recording and training. Connect the two over the existing local Wi-Fi network. Use the one robot power source specified by the power review; a data connection to the Mac does not power the robot in this route.

This reuses the current controller and preserves an onboard-compute migration path. Replacing the Pi entirely is technically possible, but needs a different camera and a new IMU transport/firmware path. A long USB cable alone does not implement that replacement.

The current Mac client already sends commands and heartbeats to the Pi over SSH. The new vision client must send bounded target/velocity requests through the same single owner of the motor bus. It must never stream raw unchecked servo goals around the local controller.

## What the Mac can already compute

[compute-benchmark.json](compute-benchmark.json) contains a fresh hardware-free benchmark on this Apple Silicon Mac, using the actual packaged ONNX walking/standing models and the exported trained jaw actor. Each model ran 5,000 timed iterations after warm-up with synthetic inputs and one CPU thread.

- Walking policy: 0.01988 ms median; 0.03696 ms at the 99th percentile.
- Standing policy: 0.01979 ms median; 0.03355 ms at the 99th percentile.
- Jaw actor: 0.00413 ms median; 0.00521 ms at the 99th percentile.

Actor computation fits comfortably within the 20 ms period of the existing 50 Hz controller. These measurements exclude USB round trips, IMU delivery, camera capture, visual estimation and operating-system scheduling. They establish compute feasibility, not a tested physical control loop. Reproduce with `benchmark_compute.py` in the existing clean environment `/tmp/microduck-phase1-clean-venv`.

The original jaw training records 6,144 decisions and 12/12 held-out simulated successes, with the scripted baseline also achieving 12/12. Its 20 inputs include simulated target position, contact forces, support and lift. The existing exported actor is therefore usable for continuation and simulator regression; camera installation alone does not turn those simulated inputs into measurements. Sources: `../../microduck-simulator/rl/results/validation.json` and `../../microduck-simulator/rl/README.md`, reached through `../../PROJECT-CONTEXT.json` from this build-pack root.

## Every connector in the recommended route

**Pi power.** Feed regulated 5 V through the Pi's header using two short pre-crimped female leads on physical pins 2 and 4, and two matching ground leads on physical pins 6 and 14. Join the two positive leads to the protected Pi branch and the two ground leads to the distribution ground. The header supplies metal pins; the finished wires supply the mating female sockets. The power review defines the accepted lead length, wire gauge, terminal rating and fuse. This removes USB power-plug fabrication and leaves the Pi receptacle marked `PWR IN` empty. Do not connect another supply to that empty receptacle or the 3.3 V pins.

The [official Raspberry Pi HAT design guide](https://github.com/raspberrypi/hats/blob/master/designguide.md) explicitly permits power through header pins 2/4 and ground, with 4.75–5.25 V at the Pi. This changes only the power connection: camera, USB host data and the existing IMU code are unaffected. Secure and label the four leads against a one-pin insertion offset. These are power pins, not programmable GPIO signal outputs. The connection requires the already-listed installed 40-pin header. Use one non-stacking 2×20 insulated female housing containing all 12 allocated power, IMU and audio contacts; it replaces the individual Pi-side plug housings. Match every contact to that housing. The completed housing and lead exit must fit the reviewed 7 × 54 × 15 mm receiving envelope; an 18 mm-high housing interferes with the motor/support and is rejected. This envelope is a design allowance, not a supplier dimension claim. The fresh mechanical reach check rejects the earlier 100 mm power leads; the power review now selects four 250 mm 22 AWG leads, screening at 4.818 V at the Pi under its explicit resistance allowances.

An alternative finished [150 mm CviLux USB-A-to-Micro-B cable](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/cvilux-usa/DH-20M50057/13177527), $1.18, uses 26/30 AWG conductors. Its [manufacturer drawing](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/3807/DH-20M50057_Dwg.pdf) is available. Adding the solderless [Adafruit 3629 screw-terminal USB-A receptacle](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3629), $4.95, would add 50 × 20 × 11.1 mm and 12 g. This route was rejected by the combined power/mechanical review because of endpoint voltage margin and unnecessary bulk; do not add these two items to the selected order.

**Pi to OpenRB-150.** The Pi supplies another Micro-USB receptacle marked `USB`. Insert the Micro-B plug of the Raspberry Pi SC0727 OTG adapter into it. This short adapter supplies a USB-A receptacle. Insert the USB-A plug of the existing Adafruit 4472 cable into that receptacle. Insert the USB-C plug at the other end of the 4472 cable into the USB-C receptacle on the OpenRB-150. All three joins plug together; none needs soldering. The Pi is the USB host and OpenRB is the peripheral. USB carries motor instructions and feedback in both directions, plus OpenRB logic power. High-current motor power follows the separate protected 5 V distribution and bypasses the controller's USB supply path.

[SC0727](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/raspberry-pi/SC0727/15298144) is $1.54, 80 mm long and had 1,414 units available. [4472](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/adafruit-industries-llc/4472/11587359) is a 152 mm data cable at $2.95. This complete data link costs $4.49. SC0727 remains borrow-first under the current sourcing policy; these prices do not release that hold. Adafruit's [1099 OTG adapter](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1099) is a confirmed compatible-function alternative at $2.50, but is longer and costs more.

ROBOTIS specifies that its [OpenRB-150](https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/parts/controller/openrb-150/) uses USB-C and the factory `usb_to_dynamixel` bridge supports 1 Mbps. Its USB source has a 500 mA fuse; ROBOTIS explicitly directs dynamic motor loads to external power. Keep the selected power jumper arrangement and the separate motor harness from the system wiring plan. Never feed the fifteen motors through the Pi or Mac USB cable.

**Camera.** Keep Raspberry Pi Camera Module 3 Wide, SC1224, and camera cable SC1892, 200 mm. Both circuit boards provide locking flat-flex sockets: the cable provides exposed contact ends, with 15 contacts at the camera and 22 contacts at the Pi Zero CSI socket. Release each latch, insert the correct contact side fully, and close the latch. No soldering or USB conversion is involved. The [official camera cable page](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/camera-cable/) explicitly covers Pi Zero models. [SC1224](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/raspberry-pi/SC1224/17278644) is $35 and [SC1892](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/raspberry-pi/SC1892/21658272) is $1. The camera receives power through its ribbon cable. Preserve the existing front camera carrier, check that the additional 50 mm of ribbon forms a gentle service loop, and retain full neck travel without tension.

**Orientation sensor.** Keep the BNO085 and extend the existing Adafruit 4397 I2C harness to a measured 250 mm: the fresh moving-body reach calculation exceeds its original 150 mm length before service slack. Retain the JST-SH end and approximately 100 mm of original wire, and splice four compatible pre-crimp signal tails to the correct final length. Insulate each splice and secure a flexible neck loop. The four tails go into the unified Pi housing, not into a second plug over that housing. Its SDA and SCL connect to Pi GPIO 2 and GPIO 3, physical header pins 3 and 5. Use physical pin 1 for the existing 3.3 V module supply and physical pin 9 for its ground, leaving the parallel Pi power returns on pins 6 and 14 free. The manufacturer colors are red for 3.3 V, blue for SDA, yellow for SCL and black for ground. Check end-to-end continuity, absence of shorts and fresh I2C reports at the actual configured bus speed. The board must stay rigidly mounted to the torso in the calibrated orientation. Existing code opens Linux `/dev/i2c-*` devices and uses Linux I2C ioctl values; this is the specific platform dependency that an ordinary Mac USB cable cannot replace.

**Audio.** Use the existing club-sourced 24 AWG pair directly from the 0.5 A fused 5 V/common ground to amplifier VIN/GND, with a slack service loop, insulated joints and strain relief. Omit the optional 1131 JST-PH connector. Head servicing requires disconnecting these direct supply joints. Keep the Adafruit 3006 MAX98357A amplifier and the 8-ohm, 1-watt Same Sky CMS-30204-18SP speaker. Pi GPIO 18, physical pin 12, connects to amp BCLK; GPIO 19, physical pin 35, to LRCLK; GPIO 21, physical pin 40, to DIN. These are the [manufacturer's Pi connections](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-max98357-i2s-class-d-mono-amp/raspberry-pi-wiring). They do not conflict with the orientation sensor's GPIO 2/3 I2C bus. The amplifier receives fused 5 V and common ground from distribution, with a signal-ground connection to Pi physical pin 20. Its two speaker screw terminals connect to the speaker's two solder pads; solder the speaker wires at those pads, then provide strain relief. Neither speaker terminal connects to ground. A 100-kilohm resistor from GAIN to VIN selects 3 dB gain; impose a volume ceiling appropriate for the 1-watt speaker. [Amplifier pinout](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-max98357-i2s-class-d-mono-amp/pinouts).

**Mac communication.** The Mac and Pi use the same local Wi-Fi network. The Pi's only USB data port is occupied by OpenRB, so it cannot simultaneously act as a USB-network gadget connected directly to the Mac through that port. Wi-Fi avoids an additional robot data tether. A wired Ethernet conversion would require a USB hub and network adapter and is outside this selected low-cost route.

## Software work that completes the visual path

1. Keep the existing balance and IMU processes on the Pi. Add camera capture using the official [Raspberry Pi camera stack](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/camera_software.html). Start with a bounded 640 × 480 or 1280 × 720 stream. Preserve capture timestamps and frame numbers; use a queue of one newest frame so old frames never accumulate. The documented H.264 network stream establishes a transport option. A timestamped JPEG frame transport is easier for the first estimator and must be measured under the actual Pi/network load.
2. On the Mac, calibrate camera intrinsics and the camera-to-mouth transform. Implement an OpenCV locator for the initially selected physical target using shape/color segmentation on a calibrated test surface. Export the target center and pose estimate, confidence, frame sequence and observation age. This is an initial target-specific visual controller and a baseline for reinforcement learning; it preserves automatic finding and alignment rather than asking the operator to place the object in the mouth.
3. Send bounded forward/yaw/head requests into the Pi's existing supervisor; keep the 50 Hz joint loop local. Add rejection of duplicate frames, stale targets, missing targets and uncalibrated coordinates before any approach. Stop when the target disappears or the communication stream expires. The existing runtime already checks a 600 ms command timeout, a 100 ms loop deadline and IMU freshness, and programs servo bus watchdogs; vision-specific freshness is an additional check.
4. Define a measured observation interface before deploying the jaw actor. Joint encoders, IMU and camera supply much of the state. Motor current is a load estimate and is not a direct substitute for two opposing fingertip contact forces. Either add calibrated contact sensing or continue training with observations available on this hardware and simulated sensor noise/occlusion. Retain the existing actor, traces and 12-condition regression as the baseline; do not silently fill unavailable force inputs with zeros and label it transferred training.
5. Record camera frames, estimated target pose, all fifteen joint states, motor currents, actions and outcome timestamps. Evaluate pickup success from observed lift/hold/release relative to the calibrated support surface. Use held-out targets/placements and failed grasps to continue the learning experiment. A camera view of only the robot's face is insufficient: the camera carrier must see the approach surface and the object through the required lift range, or use an external evaluation camera already available for independent scoring.

The hardware architecture supports all of this work; the current runtime does not yet implement steps 1–5 as an integrated visual learner. Bench commissioning and visual-task acceptance stay distinct.

## Full Mac alternative and why it is not selected

A complete no-Pi architecture needs a USB UVC camera on the duck, a short local connection from BNO085 to OpenRB's I2C pins, custom OpenRB firmware that returns timestamped IMU samples alongside motor-bus communication, a Mac driver for that protocol, and a separate sound path. The Mac runs visual estimation and the 50 Hz policy. OpenRB retains a local expiry timer so a stalled Mac cannot leave an unlimited stale command active. Motor power continues to come from the robot supply, never from the Mac USB port. The independent emergency power disconnect stays in the power harness.

OpenRB has one I2C interface and a SAMD21 controller, so its existing board can provide the missing IMU bridge without buying another computer. The existing factory firmware forwards DYNAMIXEL traffic only; it does not supply this combined sensor protocol. Replacing it also means the current rustypot transport cannot remain unchanged. This is a finite firmware/driver task, not a cable-only substitution.

The camera must use USB because the Mac has no Pi CSI socket. An in-stock DigiKey candidate is [Arducam B043301 at $84.44](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/arducam/B043301/29358214), with 18 available on this check. The manufacturer's [selection guide](https://docs.arducam.com/UVC-Camera/USB2-UVC-Camera-Kit/Specs-and-Selection-Guide/) identifies the B0433 family as a USB, manually focused wide-angle camera. Lower-cost candidates include [B0633 at $48.50](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/arducam/B0633/29513276), a color VGA global-shutter camera; its [manufacturer page](https://www.arducam.com/global-shutter-usb2-uvc-camera-ultra-high-fps-vga-color-wide-angle-arducam-ezbox-gs.html) confirms UVC. Those candidates require their own carrier and near-mouth optical fit check. They are not proposed as drop-in replacements for the selected Pi camera.

For the OpenRB connection, [StarTech USB2CC2MNC](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/startech-com/USB2CC2MNC/23350821) is a finished 2 m USB-C-to-USB-C data cable at $11.56. One plug enters a Mac USB-C port and the other enters OpenRB's USB-C port. The USB camera needs its manufacturer cable and a matching Mac adapter or USB-C hub if that cable terminates in USB-A. Audio can initially use the Mac's speaker; sound emitted by the duck instead requires a USB or analog audio path and its own amplifier/wiring. This further increases tether count.

The B0633 candidate plus the OpenRB cable alone is $60.06; adding the retained BNO085 and harness reaches $90.51 before a camera-to-Mac adapter, sound hardware and the new camera carrier. The selected Pi route is $79.70 for its listed compute/sensor/audio merchandise, with held Pi/card/header and club leads supplied separately. This comparison does not prove the Pi route is universally cheapest; it shows that removing a borrowed Pi has no demonstrated savings in the selected configurations, and causes additional firmware, optics and audio integration work. Therefore the Pi-plus-Mac architecture is selected for this redesign.

## Acceptance before operation

The current task can close hardware design after the parent integrates the new power route, exact component quantities, wire lengths and mechanical clearance. The following claims need the assembled hardware and are not established by the actor benchmark: simultaneous camera/control timing on the Pi, actual Wi-Fi observation age, near-field camera visibility, safe physical stop behavior, current/voltage/temperature under motion, and autonomous pickup success. Their tests require the ordered servos, actual Pi/camera, assembled harness and calibrated body. They must run before enabling unattended operation or claiming a successful visual pickup experiment.
