# Power redesign review

Verified 2026-09-13. Advances P5 and P6. This is a construction and commissioning design review, not a claim that unassembled hardware has passed. Preserve the camera-guided learned-pickup goal in `../../PROJECT-CONTEXT.json`; current sourcing policy remains independent of engineering requirements; the camera/audio integration holds are now released for preparation, while all user Pi/club holds remain. No purchase or cart mutation was performed for this review.

## Recommended route

Reuse the owned Xiaomi MDY-14-ED charger and its original cable. Receive **20 V USB Power Delivery at up to 3.25 A**, carry that voltage along a **2 m, two-conductor, 18 AWG copper tether**, and put the existing DFR1202 5 V converter on the robot. Keep the high-current 5 V wiring short. The same converter and all downstream wiring can later receive power from one removable onboard USB-C power bank through the same input connector.

The supported design envelope is **9 A at 5 V for the assembled robot, with a 9.5 A short-peak commissioning point**. Reserve **2 A** within that total for Pi, camera, controller, inertial sensor and audio; the corresponding motor allocation is 7 A working and 7.5 A short peak. These are design and test allocations, not measured load or a physical current limiter. Do not infer a required gait current from them.

The exact-model first-hand protocol test identifies a modified USB-A socket and original cable, with fixed USB PD profiles including 20 V / 3.25 A. Xiaomi's official product family description separately limits general-protocol operation to 65 W and requires its original cable. The printed 120 W capability belongs to the phone-specific charging path; this design does not need it. [Exact MDY-14-ED teardown and protocol measurements](https://www.chongdiantou.com/archives/206353.html), [Xiaomi official charging compatibility](https://www.mi.com/tw/product/xiaomi-120w-charging-combo-type-a/).

## Every physical connection

1. **Wall to charger:** the charger's existing mains prongs plug into an ordinary wall outlet or an existing extension strip. No mains wiring is modified.
2. **Charger to PD receiver:** the original Xiaomi cable's USB-A male plug goes into the charger's USB-A female socket. Its USB-C male plug goes into the Adafruit 5991 board's USB-C female socket. Both ends are finished connectors. Do not cut or replace this proprietary cable with an arbitrary A-to-C lead.
3. **PD receiver setup:** turn only the 20 V DIP switch on; leave the other voltage switches off. The ISET jumper must be open for the documented 3.25 A selection. No I2C wiring is needed for this route. Keep the board's STEMMA and USB data pads unused and insulated from the power wiring. Adafruit documents the switches, output screw terminal and ISET behavior. [5991 pinout and setup](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-usb-type-c-power-delivery-switchable-breakout?view=all).
4. **Receiver to long power tether:** clamp a red 18 AWG wire in the board's `+` output terminal and a black wire in `−`. The 5991 provides the screw terminal; these are stripped wire ends, not USB plugs. Put an inline 5 A fuse in the positive lead immediately after the receiver. Insulate it in the receiver's external enclosure. Retain an existing appropriately rated fuse from club inventory before buying another.
5. **Tether to robot:** fit the DFRobot FIT0586 XT30 female-contact half at the robot end of the source tether, and its male-contact half on a short robot input pigtail. Solder the two wires into each half’s two solder cups, observe the molded polarity marks, cover all four joints with heat shrink, and label the connector **20 V INPUT**. The energized source side has recessed female contacts; the robot supplies the mounted male half. Both halves need strain relief. The connector’s recommended 18 AWG wire is adequate for this 3.25 A high-voltage path.
6. **Robot input to converter:** solder the short red 18 AWG pigtail to the DFR1202 `VIN+` pad and the black pigtail to input ground. This board has solder pads, not an included plug or wire socket. Keep this pigtail under 100 mm. No raw 20 V wire goes to a servo, Pi or USB power pin.
7. **Converter to distribution:** solder 16 AWG red and black leads to `5 V OUT` and output ground, no more than 100 mm to the motor distribution. The converter is the single common 5 V source. Feed the Pi/audio branches from the converter's output star point, so motor current does not travel through a logic branch.
8. **Motor distribution:** retain the existing 10 A main motor fuse and eight 3 A fused groups, covering seven pairs plus the jaw. Use eight 250 mm 20 AWG group feeds each including 20 mm before its fuse and 230 mm after it, plus independent original 90 mm servo tails; retain DATA-only inter-servo links and common ground. Do not route the total motor current through the OpenRB servo power connector.
9. **Pi power, preferred header route:** use two short pre-crimped female wires from the fused 5 V branch to physical header pins **2 and 4**, and two short ground wires to physical pins **6 and 14**. The Pi supplies the already-soldered male header; the cable supplies the female sockets. Use 22 AWG, 250 mm one way, and fixed insulated housings that prevent moving the connections one pin sideways. These are the dedicated power pins on the header, not programmable 3.3 V GPIO signal pins. Leave the Pi's USB power socket empty. Pi-to-OpenRB uses a separate finished OTG data cable. Raspberry Pi's own add-on-board specification supports this 5 V header input arrangement and its audio product documentation demonstrates header power. [Official HAT electrical requirements](https://github.com/raspberrypi/hats), [official audio-board power documentation](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/accessories/audio.html).

**Historical short bench alternative only (not the selected onboard route):** one Adafruit 1826 Micro-B male plug with only two 20 AWG power wires soldered to its large tabs, at most 100 mm one way: pin 1 to the fused 5 V branch, pin 5 to ground; pins 2, 3 and 4 unconnected. This plug inserts into the Pi's existing female **PWR IN** socket; nothing is soldered to the Pi. The current regulator-to-Pi geometry needs 154–162 mm before termination and slack, so this 100 mm alternative does not reach and is excluded from the current build. It also has less voltage margin than the parallel header route. Do not install both power routes. [Adafruit connector construction](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/1014/1826_Web.pdf).

A single 2 m electrical tether is sufficient for the stated test area; there is no need to buy a very long proprietary charging cable. Support the tether above the robot with a loose service loop and strain relief so its weight and drag do not become a new balance disturbance. If 1 m already reaches when the extension strip sits near the lane, the external receiver can be near the lane and the ordinary tether can be shortened.

## Capacity and voltage calculations

The accompanying [power-calculation.json](power-calculation.json) evaluates 20 combinations of source voltage, converter efficiency and robot load. It uses an upper output tolerance of 5.05 V, an engineering source-voltage range of 19–20 V, an efficiency range of 85–90%, and an acceptance limit of 0.40 ohm for the complete upstream round trip, including the original USB cable, receiver path, connectors and tether. These bounds must be checked during commissioning; they are not measured properties of the user's equipment.

At the most demanding modeled corner, 19 V and 85% efficiency:

- 9 A robot output requires 3.004 A from the charger; source power is 57.081 W.
- 9.5 A robot output requires 3.184 A; source power is 60.496 W, below the 3.25 A source limit.
- 10 A requires 3.365 A and is rejected. This is why the design does not silently round the available capacity up to 10 A.

The two 2 m 18 AWG wires have a modeled 40 °C round-trip resistance of 0.09039 ohm. At 3 A they lose about 0.271 V and 0.813 W. The same pair carrying 9 A at 5 V would lose 0.813 V and 7.321 W. Carrying 20 V to the robot and converting locally therefore materially improves both delivered voltage and tether heating. A 16 AWG tether also fits the electrical budget (0.05682 ohm and 0.511 W at 3 A), but costs additional mass and may need 18 AWG pigtails at the XT30’s recommended wire size. Keep 16 AWG for the short, high-current 5 V distribution.

For the Pi's 1.5 A branch, the preferred pair of positive and pair of ground 250 mm 22 AWG header wires screen at **4.818 V** from the converter's 4.95 V lower tolerance. This allows 20 milliohm per individual header connection (parallel paths give 20 milliohm total supply-contact round trip), 10 milliohm for joints, and the existing fuse's hot-drop equivalent. Require known contact ratings of at least 1.5 A per connection and verify even current sharing/retention; an unspecified loose jumper's appearance is not evidence of its rating. The 22 AWG 250 mm parallel pair has 14.29 milliohm wire-loop resistance at 40 degrees C and screens at 4.818 V; its conductor loss is essentially the same as the rejected-for-reach 26 AWG 100 mm calculation. The factory contact must explicitly accept 22 AWG; do not force 20 AWG into an unrated 2.54 mm terminal. A historical short Micro-B bench alternative screens at 4.769 V using a 60 milliohm contact-pair allowance, but does not reach the current robot placement. Actual endpoint voltage remains an acceptance measurement. A 150 mm 26 AWG finished lead plus a USB-A socket adds resistance and loses this margin, so the large terminal adapter is rejected for this compact build. Do not route all 2 A of non-motor load through the Pi power input: audio receives its own branch.

The fresh full-motion reach check rejects the old 150 mm motor branch wires. Use 250 mm 20 AWG per group and keep each original 90 mm servo tail independent. Eight groups consume 2.0 m per color before cutting/splicing allowance, so allocate 2.5 m red and 2.5 m black from club stock. Using the inherited 5 milliohm joint allowances, 20 milliohm per servo contact, hot fuse equivalents, a 100 mm 16 AWG motor trunk, and an extra 20 mV output-ripple allowance, the motor endpoint screens at **4.311 V** with 7 A aggregate motors, 2.25 A in the loaded group and 1.3 A at its loaded servo. At 7.5 A aggregate short peak it screens at **4.285 V**. A single stalled pair at 2.94 A group and 1.47 A each still screens at **4.220 V**, but is a fault to stop, not a sustained operating target. The original tail is 21 AWG; calculation conservatively uses the higher resistance of 22 AWG. These results exceed the 4.0 V operating screen; all contact/joint and thermal bounds still require assembly measurements. Applying the same extra 20 mV reserve to the Pi gives 4.798 V.

ROBOTIS specifies 1.47 A stalled current per XL330-M288 at 5 V, 17 mA standby and a 3.7–6.0 V input range, with 5 V recommended. Fifteen simultaneous stalls total 22.05 A before logic. That is a fault state to stop, not a legitimate sustained operating mode. The manual's Current Limit register applies to current control and current-based position control; it does not establish a current ceiling in ordinary position mode. Reducing it also reduces torque. A mode change therefore requires retesting the trained control adapter rather than silently imposing an unverified low-torque gait. [ROBOTIS XL330 specification and control table](https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/x/xl330-m288/).

## Converter behavior and protection

DFR1202 is specified for 6–24 V input, 5 V ±1% output, 16 A maximum output, output short-circuit protection and 125 °C internal over-temperature protection. Its documented greater-than-90% efficiency condition uses a 12 V input; the 20 V route therefore uses an 85% design floor rather than copying the 12 V result. Its package is 30 × 26 × 8 mm. The rear thermal pad is intended to couple to copper for heat removal. Use the open lower-body carrier and available copper heat-spreading material; do not foam-tape over the rear pad or claim free-air 16 A continuous output inside the robot. [DFRobot module specification](https://www.dfrobot.com/product-2907.html).

The source and module have short-circuit protection, while the fuses protect wiring. Neither a fuse rating nor the 9 A operating budget guarantees that a faulty group alone will disconnect or that the Pi will stay running. The available module documentation does not specify an output overvoltage cutoff, reverse-current blocker, or regenerative energy absorption rating. Accordingly, the construction uses one source, no parallel 5 V supplies, staged torque enable and a physical power disconnect. A Mac data connection must not join a second USB 5 V supply to this rail; the compute design must keep or explicitly isolate its power domain. With header power, fit a blanking plug or cover over Pi PWR IN to prevent accidentally attaching a second source; the official HAT safety guidance explicitly calls out this coexistence issue.

Do not add a generic “5 V TVS diode” as a claimed 5.5 V clamp: its high-current clamp voltage can exceed the servo's range. Do not claim that a large capacitor solves regeneration without an energy and transient check. The existing parts can be tested as assembled; if a stop/release transient exceeds the acceptance voltage, the positive repair route is a properly specified active clamp or a converter designed to absorb that energy, followed by the same transient test.

Startup procedure: motors disabled, camera and audio idle, connect source, verify about 20 V at the receiver and 4.95–5.05 V at the converter before connecting loads, boot logic, then enable joint groups gradually from calibrated positions. For normal source changes, disable torque, shut down the Pi, unplug the two-pole 20 V connector, and then remove the power module or bank. Pulling the same connector is the immediate whole-robot emergency disconnect. The computer's control-stop/watchdog remains a separate firmware responsibility.

## Parts and consolidation

Keep the existing Adafruit 5991 ($9.95) and DFR1202 ($12.90) selection; the change from 12 V to 20 V does not require a second converter or battery. The existing 10 A motor fuse and nine 3 A branch fuses remain reusable. The input-fuse addition is one installed 5 A Littelfuse 0251005.MXL, DigiKey F2317-ND; one working unit costs $1.24; no spare is purchased. The latest product-page check showed 4,966 in stock. [5 A input fuse](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/littelfuse-inc/0251005-MXL/700749).

The detachable power connector is one DFRobot FIT0586 XT30 male/female pair, DigiKey 1738-1436-ND, **$1.90 merchandise**, with 19,240 in stock at the check. The vendor specifies 15 A continuous, 500 V and 0.70 milliohm contact resistance, so its rating comfortably exceeds this 20 V / 3.25 A input. The package contains both mating halves; it needs four soldered wire-to-cup joints. This replaces the larger $5.12 Powerpole assembly considered during the review. [DigiKey pair](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/dfrobot/FIT0586/9559255), [DFRobot manufacturer specification](https://media.digikey.com/pdf/Data%20Sheets/DFRobot%20PDFs/FIT0586_Web.pdf).

Club-first requirements are 2 m each red/black 18 AWG flexible copper wire plus short 16 AWG internal 5 V leads, heat shrink, strain relief, mounting material, fuses/connector equivalents where available, and soldering/testing tools. A generic breadboard or thin jumper lead is not a substitute for the high-current distribution. Do not buy these merely because the engineering design calls for them.

The whole order still needs the parent procurement review to combine camera/audio/data cabling changes, shipping, tariff and held-item status. The $1.90 above is deliberately a priced connector assembly, not a delivered-order total.

## Replace the wall source with one onboard power bank

Retain the same 20 V input pigtail, converter and downstream harness. A future removable tray carries a bank with a documented single-port **20 V / at least 3.25 A fixed USB PD output**, the 5991 board, and a short appropriately rated USB-C cable. Unplug the wall tether and plug in that bank module; they must never be paralleled. A 65 W or higher nameplate is insufficient if its single-port fixed 20 V profile does not meet the requirement. If using a standard USB-C cable above 3 A, it needs 5 A electronic identification.

Capacity, mass and tray dimensions remain mechanical acceptance requirements. The current PB1033MI is neither electrically equivalent to the 65 W source nor proven to fit. Expansion means an interchangeable source interface, not a promise that any future bank fits or that adding its mass leaves balance unchanged.

## Finite commissioning acceptance

The available calculations support this source and wiring architecture, replacing the rejected 30 W arrangement. No physical test has yet been performed; the exact external action is to assemble the already-defined chain after the servo/controller and electrical parts arrive, then run these checks with borrowed measuring equipment:

1. Verify the original Xiaomi cable and receiver actually establish 20 V. Check polarity before inserting robot power.
2. Apply controlled loads at 5.5, 8 and 9 A, with a 9.5 A short pulse. Verify source current stays below 3.25 A, complete upstream resistance is at most 0.40 ohm, and efficiency at 20 V is at least 85%. Reject 10 A as the guaranteed design envelope.
3. At the chosen 9 A continuous point, record 30 minutes of converter, connector and cable temperatures. The assembly must settle without protection cycling; use the project's 60 °C accessible-case criterion. Improve the heat spreader and repeat if necessary.
4. At the Pi pins and furthest servos, capture boot, torque-enable, walk, beak close/lift/release and emergency-stop voltage. Require Pi 4.75–5.25 V and the project motor operating band 4.0–5.5 V; use the scope to detect brief overshoots.
5. Validate the trained-task runtime with camera and maximum allowed audio enabled. Log actual aggregate current and each servo's reported input current. If normal task peaks exceed the power envelope, reduce verified acceleration/current without losing pickup/balance performance, or swap the external source for a documented higher-current PD source and explicitly reconfigure the receiver. A change in receiver configuration alone cannot turn this Xiaomi unit's fixed 65 W PD profile into 100 W.

This closes the design question positively: the owned charger supplies a practical, higher-margin single-source construction with an interchangeable future bank input. Only assembled-device behavior remains dependent on the physical parts; it is not evidence of a finished visual learned-pickup robot.
