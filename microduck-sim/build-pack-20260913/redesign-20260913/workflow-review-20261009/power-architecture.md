# Central power without an external fuse array

Verified 9 October 2026. Problems P5/P6. This is an architecture review, not an energized wiring release. No purchases, powered tests or canonical wiring changes were made.

**Decision:** retire the assumption that MicroDuck necessarily needs eleven individually mounted external fuses. Prefer one PB1033MI USB-C PD output, the received Adafruit 5991 receiver, and the received DFR1202 converter as the central supply candidate. Keep motor power distribution separate from the OpenRB data bus. This preserves more available power than using two bank outputs, but does not yet establish the final branch protection or full-robot current budget. A small central protection/distribution assembly is a possible implementation; an unspecified extra board is not an approved purchase.

**The proposed two-port shortcut does not supply two independent high-power rails.** It is suitable only for a deliberately limited aggregate load after bench checks. Feeding all motors through OpenRB USB is rejected. Feeding fifteen motors through one OpenRB/DYNAMIXEL power path is not accepted merely because they fit on one DATA bus.

## Source findings

- **PB1033MI:** Xiaomi specifies simultaneous multi-port output as **5 V / 3 A total (15 W)**. Each USB-C can individually offer **20 V / 1.5 A (30 W)**; the advertised 33 W includes a different voltage/protocol operating point. Thus a second load cannot be assumed to preserve a 20 V PD contract on the first port. The manual advises against repeated connection changes during fast charging and documents protective mode. It does not specify interruption duration, renegotiation sequence, or guaranteed seamless recovery. These are unmeasured, not zero. [Official specifications](https://www.mi.com/global/product/xiaomi-33w-power-bank-10000-integrated-cable/specs/), [official manual](sources/pb1033mi-manual.pdf).

- **OpenRB-150:** USB supplies 5 V; terminal/VIN accepts 3.7–12.6 V. The documented DYNAMIXEL-port current rating is **3 A**, with no published basis here for treating it as 3 A per port. USB has a nominal **500 mA fuse**; ROBOTIS recommends terminal power for rapid or high-torque movement. Set the terminal configuration to **VIN(DXL)** with power removed. The motor FET defaults off on startup/reset. The accessory 5 V pin is only 150 mA on the updated regulator, not a Pi supply. [Official manual](https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/parts/controller/openrb-150/).

- **OpenRB protection circuit:** the actual official schematic puts **F1 MF-MSMF050-2 only in USB VBUS**, ahead of D3. Terminal J4/XT60 J5 reaches MAIN_VCC through JP1 and reaches DXL_PWR through SQS401EN. No motor-rail current-sense limiter or second series fuse is shown. The FET is a commanded switch, not evidence of automatic branch overcurrent protection. This is circuit interpretation of Rev B sheet 2/3, internally dated 6 March 2025, although the downloadable filename says 20231130. The board in hand may differ; inspect its revision. [ROBOTIS download entry](https://www.robotis.com/service/download.php?no=2117), [saved schematic](sources/openrb-150-schematic-20231130.pdf).

- **The 500 mA component is a resettable PTC, not a precise 500 mA clamp.** Bourns lists 0.50 A hold and 1.00 A trip for MF-MSMF050 under its specified conditions. Do not use its nominal value to promise instantaneous shutdown or tolerate a multi-amp motor load. [Manufacturer datasheet](https://www.bourns.com/docs/product-datasheets/mfmsmf.pdf).

- **XL330-M288:** operating range 3.7–6 V, recommended 5 V; published stall current **1.47 A at 5 V**, standby 17 mA. Fifteen simultaneous stall currents sum to **22.05 A**. That is a stress reference, not a forecast of normal walking consumption. Current-control settings constrain supported motor-control modes; they do not protect a cable short ahead of the actuator. Temperature/voltage shutdown is likewise not a branch-wire fuse. [ROBOTIS actuator specification](https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/x/xl330-m288/).

- **Motor connector bottleneck:** JST rates the EH family **3 A with AWG22**. A DATA daisy chain and a high-current VDD trunk are different constraints. Reuse genuine motor leads, but do not route the combined fifteen-actuator load through one EH power contact based on data-address capacity. [JST EH datasheet](https://www.jst-mfg.com/product/pdf/eng/eEH.pdf).

- **DFR1202:** input **6–24 V**, output **5 V ±1%**, maximum output **16 A**, with output-short-circuit and 125°C overtemperature protection. Published >90% efficiency is tied to a 12 V test condition. The reviewed documentation does not specify the short-circuit trip current/time or a user-settable current limit. Consequently “16 A protected converter” is not proof that every smaller lead is protected. At 20 V / 1.5 A input, even ideal conversion cannot exceed 6 A at 5 V; assuming 90% gives an illustrative 5.4 A shared by all loads, not an accepted 20 V efficiency result. [DFRobot specification](https://www.dfrobot.com/product-2907.html).

- **Received PD receiver:** product 5991 is the switchable HUSB238 version, not the older solder-jumper-only 5807. Requesting 20 V does not force an incapable source to provide it. Confirm the actual negotiated voltage before enabling the converter/load. A fallback to 5 V is below DFR1202's specified input range. [Adafruit 5991](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5991), [its guide](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-usb-type-c-power-delivery-switchable-breakout?view=all).

## What the alternatives actually change

**Two outputs, direct 5 V:** one feeds the Pi, the other supplies OpenRB terminal power through a correctly terminated cable. This avoids the converter and can separate some wiring, but both loads share the bank's 15 W budget. It is not electrical isolation, independent current budgets, or a way to double capacity. USB data connections must not accidentally join output VBUS rails. Pi logic and motor pulses may still cause a shared reset. It is a reasonable low-load bring-up candidate, not a verified full-motion supply.

**One direct 5 V output:** also limited to 15 W before losses, with one central split and fewer bank-port state changes. It has no power advantage over the two-port configuration, but is simpler if measured total demand fits. The same connector and fault-protection limits remain.

**One 20 V PD output into the existing converter — preferred candidate:** potentially about twice the input power of multi-port mode, one negotiation, and no second bank port to change operating mode. Use only this bank output while operating the robot. Split the regulated 5 V at a central distribution point; deliver motor current through appropriately rated regional feeds, and connect OpenRB DATA/GND without creating an unintended parallel motor-power path. The exact feed grouping and protection are still to be selected against measured load and fault limits. Do not claim a fixed number of new branches before that evidence exists.

If external motor injection bypasses the OpenRB FET, an OpenRB reset no longer disconnects those motors. A central, physically accessible motor-power disconnect and a tested lost-command stop are therefore part of this candidate, not something the existing FET implicitly supplies. Never connect the 20 V receiver output directly to OpenRB or an XL330.

## Why OMX does not establish a need for eleven fuses — or permission to remove all protection

OMX demonstrates that a compact robot harness need not expose a separate fuse at every actuator. It is useful precedent for packaging and assembly. Its documented hardware comprises a six-axis 5 V leader and a separate mixed-actuator follower, with a converter and separate supply hardware; that is not this fifteen-XL330 moving robot and sensor load. Its photos do not establish hidden protection thresholds. Reuse the clarity of its wiring, not an unverified extrapolation of power capacity. [Official OMX hardware](https://docs.robotis.com/docs/systems/omx/specifications/hardware/).

There are three distinct protection questions: the bank protects its own output/battery; OpenRB protects or switches specific board paths; smaller wires and contacts need a coordinated fault-energy limit. A single central current limiter can replace multiple downstream fuses **only when its worst-case response protects every downstream conductor/contact**. Alternatively, a few protected regional outputs can be contained in one compact assembly. Neither arrangement requires eleven external inline holders. Conversely, the received 10 A main fuse or a nominal 3 A branch fuse is not automatically an adequate substitute: nominal current is not a time-current curve or an instantaneous clamp.

## Existing material and finite acceptance

The received-order record confirms **5991 receiver, DFR1202, XT30 disconnect, one 10 A fuse, one 5 A fuse, nine 3 A fuses and one 0.5 A fuse**. They are inventory, not a requirement to install them all. PB1033MI ownership and the actual Zero W are user evidence. OpenRB/XL330 kit stock remains separate from the DigiKey receipt. No replacement source, limiter, harness or adapter is assumed already available. [Received order](../../procurement/order-101587695-received.json).

Before committing the simplified wiring, conduct these bounded, off-robot/bench checks; none was performed in this review:

1. **Source mode:** log voltage/current for single-port 20 V negotiation and an electronic-load ramp up to the published 1.5 A input limit. Separately attach/remove a second bank load while monitoring both rails; record voltage downgrade, interruption and recovery. This tests the hypothesis that dual-port operation preserves a high-power first port; the published rating already rejects that as a design assumption.
2. **Converter and usable budget:** measure output voltage, temperature and input power at representative sustained/pulsed loads. Determine a continuous and transient operating envelope with headroom. Do not label the illustrative 5.4 A calculation measured capacity.
3. **Robot demand:** measure Pi/camera/audio baseline, then supported motor motions with the robot restrained appropriately. Log voltage at the farthest actuator and Pi plus each proposed regional feed current. Avoid deliberately stalling all motors. Accept only motions within source, board, wire and contact ratings; use results to choose direct 5 V versus PD and the minimum feed grouping.
4. **Protection and stop:** on a dummy harness/load fixture, establish the chosen limiter/fuse response and maximum branch temperature against its actual conductor/contact ratings; verify polarity, VBUS non-paralleling, disconnect behavior, startup/reset and stale-command stopping. Fault tests are not performed by shorting the assembled robot.

**Release boundary:** the review supports eliminating the eleven-fuse-array requirement and selecting the single-PD central architecture for detailed verification. It does not release a newly unfused full-power harness. If a compact coordinated protection solution cannot be demonstrated from existing hardware, specify that one missing function before proposing a purchase; do not silently recreate the eleven-device array or declare the converter's unspecified protection sufficient.
