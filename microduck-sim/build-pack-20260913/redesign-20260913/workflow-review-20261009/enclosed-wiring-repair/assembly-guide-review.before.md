# Assembly guide review

Use the project's source geometry and actual guide generator. Preserve existing parts and the user's completed assembly unless evidence requires a change. Locate the original failing page, reproduce the reported defect, and repair its owning source.

Before changing geometry, establish one assembly revision and a staged review boundary. Use native CAD solids/assemblies for dimensions, constraints, component interference and tool/plug corridors; export the viewer from that revision. Bind named electrical ports, harness routes and assembly operations to the same component IDs and transforms. A rendered line between part centres is schematic connectivity, not a qualified cable route. Compare source, adopted and proposed configurations without overwriting released print files.

Sequence dependent acceptance as packaging/power, one complete wired subassembly, integrated motion, local fit prototypes and incremental manufacture. Independent port registration and local access checks may proceed in parallel; rerun only geometry affected by the chosen architecture. Show useful intermediate CAD results when the user requests staged review. A failed restricted flexible-curve search alone does not justify a rigid-part redesign. Use dimensions and affected pairs to reject bad concepts before full-scene checks. Source/converter and wire ratings determine electrical protection; another robot's visible hardware does not establish equivalent protection.

1. Build the operation dependency chain: parts already present, loose/retained state, joint pose, complete moving group, fasteners, tool side, connector work, and the next closure that removes access. Check across guide sections. Assemble internal screws and connectors before their access closes; use a real off-robot subassembly when appropriate. If opening a joint resolves an access conflict, move its attached parts together and verify the angular path and tool clearance in that pose.
2. Validate displayed transforms against actual obstacles throughout the motion. Final fit cannot validate the approach. Move every attached part together. Check both mirrored assemblies independently. Isolation/transparency are inspection controls and cannot remove physical obstacles from the test.
3. Check screw insertion and driver/nut-holding access separately. Declare shaft diameter, usable length, head/handle space, and any short or right-angle tool required. Confirm access before installing the part that blocks it.
4. For cables, identify both connectors and orientation, planned length, free-end threading order, openings/clips, joint slack and closure deadline. Look up the exact purchased SKU, manufacturer drawing and revision before asking for measurements. When reviewed sources omit a required dimension, record that gap and continue work that does not depend on it; do not turn nominal length into flexible-span length or substitute an unverified narrower cable. Check the entire cable, spare length and self-contact; a ribbon must bend without in-plane stretching or shearing, not merely follow a collision-free centerline. Across joint poses, track the same material arc-length points and verify their positions and tangents change continuously. Shortest-rotation constructions can switch branches at antiparallel tangents: individually feasible sampled curves do not prove continuous motion. Retain joint increment versus maximum material-point displacement/tangent-change evidence; do not hide a jump by reindexing samples or changing segment identities. Test connector-body passage rather than only the wire curve. Keep the project's electrical topology; a reference robot's cabling does not authorize copying its power connections.
5. Separate prepare, assemble and check. Show the work face and reverse side; give a concrete observable completion condition. An exploded view must be labeled as such. If source geometry itself prevents a rigid path, show the measured stop and keep physical fit unresolved; do not replace the defect with a warning or teleport into a seated pose.
6. Bind acceptance to source meshes, operation state and rendered motion. Retain original failing traces and test restoring a bad direction or premature closure. Before treating a zero intersection as clearance, verify that both receiving solids, the intersection operation and its result are valid; read exported solids back and reject open or invalid meshes. Samples need a declared spacing; they establish sampled clearance only. Explicit pair-specific final-contact limits must not waive intermediate traversal or permit arbitrary overlap.
7. Rebuild, deploy through the existing authorized route, and inspect the original live page at start/intermediate/end, pause, scrub and module transitions. Test per-hole and per-part coverage, sequence, motion, tool access and cable-before-closure independently. Report physical fit and commissioning separately.
8. Before expensive geometry work, inspect imported helpers for scene-building side effects and measure one representative check inside the project's memory/time guard. Within each worker, evaluate one candidate, pose and obstacle at a time, and save numerical results incrementally. Use bounded parallel workers for independent measured lightweight checks when the shared resource guard permits; this is not a blanket serialization requirement. Do not retain transformed complete scenes for every pose. If the geometry allocator retains memory, use short-lived per-obstacle workers; a stopped budget calls for smaller work units, not a higher limit. Keep source hashes and failed traces. Reconcile completed records against the expected pose/obstacle inventory; timed-out, skipped and unrun items stay incomplete.
